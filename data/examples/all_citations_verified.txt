PLAINTIFF'S MEMORANDUM ON THE CONVENTION'S LIMITATIONS PERIOD AND PREEMPTIVE SCOPE (COMPOSED PASSAGE)

The plaintiff's claim arises out of personal injury sustained during international carriage by air, and it is governed by the Montreal Convention, which is the successor to the Warsaw Convention. The Supreme Court has defined the scope of the carrier's liability under the treaty. In Air France v. Saks, 470 U.S. 392 (1985), the Court held that the term "accident" in Article 17 of the Warsaw Convention means an unexpected or unusual event or happening that is external to the passenger. In Eastern Airlines, Inc. v. Floyd, 499 U.S. 530 (1991), the Court held that Article 17 does not permit recovery for mental or emotional injury that is not the result of a physical injury. In El Al Israel Airlines, Ltd. v. Tsui Yuan Tseng, 525 U.S. 155 (1999), the Court held that the Warsaw Convention provides the exclusive remedy for injuries sustained in international air travel, so that a passenger may not bring a parallel state-law claim. In Zicherman v. Korean Air Lines Co., 516 U.S. 217 (1996), the Court held that the Convention does not permit a plaintiff to recover damages for loss of society resulting from the death of a relative.

The courts of appeals have applied the same treaty framework. See Ehrlich v. American Airlines, Inc., 360 F.3d 366 (2d Cir. 2004) (addressing whether a passenger may recover damages for psychological injury under the Convention). The two-year period in Article 35 of the Montreal Convention is a condition on the right to damages and is not subject to the forum's ordinary tolling rules; the Second Circuit explained in Fishman v. Delta Air Lines, Inc., 132 F.3d 138, 144 (2d Cir. 1998), that "Almost every court that has reviewed the drafting minutes of the Convention" has rejected the contention that the treaty incorporates the tolling rules of the forum. The Eleventh Circuit reached the same result on the question of tolling while permitting an amended pleading to relate back, holding that "we agree with the consensus of courts that the Montreal Convention permits the application of Rule 15(c) relation back." Campbell v. Air Jamaica, Ltd., 760 F.3d 1165 (11th Cir. 2014). The same court had earlier set out the factors used to decide whether a passenger was engaged in the operations of embarking or disembarking. Marotte v. American Airlines, Inc., 296 F.3d 1255, 1260 (11th Cir. 2002).

The plaintiff's pleading is sufficient at this stage. A court must accept all well-pleaded factual allegations as true and draw all reasonable inferences in favor of the plaintiff. Doe v. United States, 419 F.3d 1058 (9th Cir. 2005). The district courts in this circuit have applied the Convention's two-year period to claims of exactly this kind. Mateo v. JetBlue Airways Corp., 847 F. Supp. 2d 383, 387-88 (E.D.N.Y. 2012); see also Pennington v. British Airways, 275 F. Supp. 2d 601, 606-07 (E.D. Pa. 2003) (holding that the Convention's limitations period is not subject to equitable tolling). State courts adjudicating claims that arise under the Convention apply the treaty's limitations period rather than the forum's. Kahn v. Trans World Airlines, Inc., 82 A.D.2d 696 (N.Y. App. Div. 1981). New York's own limitations and tolling rules do not govern a claim that arises under federal law. Podraza v. Carriero, 212 A.D.2d 331 (N.Y. App. Div. 1995); O'Hara v. Bayliner, 89 N.Y.2d 636 (N.Y. 1997).
