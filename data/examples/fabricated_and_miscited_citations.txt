AFFIRMATION IN OPPOSITION (RECONSTRUCTED) - Mata v. Avianca, Inc., No. 22-cv-1461 (PKC) (S.D.N.Y.)

I. State courts have concurrent jurisdiction over claims arising out of international airline accidents under the Montreal Convention.

The courts have routinely held that the Montreal Convention does not preempt state law remedies on claims arising out of international airline accidents and that state courts have concurrent jurisdiction in such instances. In Shaboon v. EgyptAir, 2013 IL App (1st) 111279-U (Ill. App. Ct. 2013), the Illinois Appellate Court held that a passenger's claim under the Montreal Convention could proceed in state court. In Petersen v. Iran Air, 905 F. Supp. 2d 121 (D.D.C. 2012), the United States District Court for the District of Columbia held that state courts have concurrent jurisdiction over claims arising out of an international airline accident, and that the plaintiff was not required to bring the claim in federal court. In Martinez v. Delta Airlines, Inc., 2019 WL 4639462 (Tex. App. Sept. 25, 2019), the plaintiff brought a negligence claim against Delta Airlines in Texas state court for injuries sustained during a flight from Amsterdam to Atlanta, and the court applied the Montreal Convention as the source of the cause of action. In Estate of Durden v. KLM Royal Dutch Airlines, 2017 WL 2418825 (Ga. Ct. App. June 5, 2017), the estate of a passenger who died on a KLM flight from Amsterdam to Atlanta brought a wrongful death claim in Georgia state court, and the court applied the Montreal Convention to the claim. The plaintiff in this action resided in New York at the time of the incident and at the time the action was commenced, and the defendant had a registered agent in New York and regularly conducted business in New York.

II. The statute of limitations is tolled by the bankruptcy of the defendant under the Montreal Convention.

The United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit addressed the effect of a bankruptcy stay under the Montreal Convention in Varghese v. China Southern Airlines Co., 925 F.3d 1339, 1349 (11th Cir. 2019), stating that "[t]he tolling effect of the automatic stay on a statute of limitations is generally a matter of federal law." That court held that the automatic stay provisions of the Bankruptcy Code may toll the statute of limitations under the Warsaw Convention, the precursor to the Montreal Convention, and saw no reason why the same rule should not apply under the Montreal Convention. In reaching that conclusion the court relied on Miller v. United Airlines, Inc., 174 F.3d 366, 371-72 (2d Cir. 1999), and on In re Air Crash Disaster Near New Orleans, 821 F.2d 1147, 1165 (5th Cir. 1987), and it cited Kaiser Steel Corp. v. W.S. Ranch Co., 391 U.S. 593 (1968), for the proposition that the tolling effect of the automatic stay on a limitations period is a matter of federal law.

In the instant case the defendant filed a petition for bankruptcy on May 10, 2020, creating an automatic stay under the Bankruptcy Code. The automatic stay therefore tolled the two-year limitations period, and this action was commenced on February 2, 2022, within the time allowed.

III. The scope of the Convention.

The Supreme Court has addressed the scope of the Convention on several occasions. In Zicherman v. Korean Air Lines Co., 516 U.S. 217 (1996), the Court held that the Warsaw Convention does not permit a plaintiff to recover damages for loss of society resulting from the death of a relative. In El Al Israel Airlines, Ltd. v. Tsui Yuan Tseng, 525 U.S. 155, 156 (1999), the Court held that the Warsaw Convention provides the exclusive remedy for injuries sustained in international air travel. See also Ehrlich v. American Airlines, Inc., 360 F.3d 366 (2d Cir. 2004) (addressing the recovery of psychological damages by a passenger in international transportation).
